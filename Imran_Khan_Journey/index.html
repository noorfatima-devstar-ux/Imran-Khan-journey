01 / The beginnings

A Lahore childhood.
A global horizon.

Before the stadiums and state halls, there was a young student discovering the discipline that would shape a public life.

Born · 5 October 1952

Imran Ahmed Khan Niazi was born in Lahore on 5 October 1952, into a family with roots in Pakistan's Pashtun community.

He attended school in Pakistan and later studied at the Royal Grammar School Worcester in England. In 1972, he began studying Philosophy, Politics and Economics at Keble College, Oxford, while developing his cricket career.

His early years crossed cultures and institutions. They helped prepare him for a life lived in the public eye, though the meaning of that life would change dramatically over time.

02 / The sporting years

A captain's belief.
A nation's moment.

For two decades, cricket made Imran Khan an international figure. The 1992 World Cup became the defining sporting chapter.

1971

International debut

Khan made his Test debut for Pakistan against England at Birmingham, beginning a long international career.

Late 1970s–1980s

The all-rounder emerges

He developed into one of the game's prominent all-rounders, known for fast bowling, batting and an increasingly influential presence in the team.

1982

Captain of Pakistan

He took on national captaincy and became a central figure in Pakistan's pursuit of consistency and major tournament success.

25 March 1992

World champions

Pakistan defeated England in the World Cup final in Melbourne. Khan, captain of the side, lifted the trophy in what became a defining national sporting memory.

1992
One defining day

Cornered Tigers.
World champions.

After a difficult start to the tournament, Pakistan rallied to win the men's Cricket World Cup. Khan's leadership and the team's collective performance made the victory a lasting part of Pakistan's sporting story.

03 / Beyond the boundary

Turning personal loss
into public service.

Healthcare and education became lasting parts of Khan's work beyond sport, with institutions built around long-term public missions.

The death of his mother from cancer helped inspire his campaign to build a cancer hospital in Pakistan.

Shaukat Khanum Memorial Cancer Hospital and Research Centre opened in Lahore in December 1994. The institution's stated mission includes providing cancer care, with financial assistance for patients who cannot afford treatment.

Khan also helped establish Namal, an education initiative in Mianwali that developed into Namal University. These projects extended his public identity from athlete to fundraiser and institutional founder.

04 / A new arena

From national hero
to political challenger.

Khan founded Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf in 1996. The party's rise was gradual, marked by early electoral setbacks and later mass mobilization.

1996

PTI is founded

Khan launched Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf, presenting an agenda that included anti-corruption and institutional reform.

1997–2008

A long period in opposition

The party struggled to win broad electoral support in its early years. Khan continued to build a political platform around accountability and governance.

2013

A breakthrough election

PTI became a major national political force and formed the provincial government in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, while emerging as a significant opposition party nationally.

2014–2018

Mass rallies and national reach

Large demonstrations, campaigning and the party's anti-corruption message helped it expand its reach ahead of the 2018 general election.

05 / In office · 2018–2022

Leading a country
through hard choices.

Khan became Pakistan's prime minister in August 2018. His government faced debt and balance-of-payments pressures, political opposition, the COVID-19 pandemic and difficult policy trade-offs.

Policy aims and government claims are not the same as independently verified outcomes. The summary below distinguishes stated priorities from the broader record.
2018

Taking office

After the July general election, Khan was elected by the National Assembly and sworn in as prime minister on 18 August 2018.

2019

Economic stabilization

The government pursued an IMF-supported programme amid external financing needs, inflation and fiscal pressures. The adjustment had economic and social costs.

2019–22

Climate and conservation

The Ten Billion Tree Tsunami programme expanded from the earlier Billion Tree Tsunami initiative. Its scale and outcomes require assessment through implementation records and independent monitoring.

2020–22

COVID-19 response

The government used a combination of restrictions, relief measures and a policy emphasis on balancing public health with livelihoods. Its approach and results were debated.

10 Apr 2022

No-confidence vote

The National Assembly passed a no-confidence motion, removing Khan from office. He was Pakistan's first prime minister removed through a parliamentary no-confidence vote.

The private person
behind the public image.

Personal context

Khan's life has moved between elite sport, public service, political leadership and intense public scrutiny.

He has been married three times: to Jemima Goldsmith, journalist Reham Khan, and Bushra Bibi. He has two sons with Goldsmith. Their lives are private, and this biography does not speculate about them.

Khan has spoken publicly about faith, national identity, social justice and his vision for Pakistan. Those statements form part of his public record; individual beliefs and private experiences should not be inferred beyond what he has shared.

09 / Achievements & legacy

One life. Many interpretations.

His impact is visible in cricket, charitable institutions and Pakistan's political conversation. The evaluation of his leadership remains contested.

1992

World Cup champion

Captained Pakistan to its first men's Cricket World Cup title, a milestone in the country's sporting history.

1994

Cancer care

Helped establish Shaukat Khanum Memorial Cancer Hospital and Research Centre, a major charitable healthcare institution.

1996

Political movement

Founded PTI, which grew from a small party into one of Pakistan's major political forces.

2018

Prime minister

Led the federal government during a period of economic strain, climate policy initiatives and the COVID-19 crisis.

Education

Namal

Supported the creation of an education institution in Mianwali, adding higher education to his philanthropic work.

Legacy

A contested record

Supporters emphasize reform, sovereignty and social programmes; critics point to governance, economic outcomes and political polarization.
